### Step 1: Key observations and facts

- If $g$ has order $15$, then the cyclic subgroup $\langle g\rangle$ has order $15$.
  
- The number of generators of a cyclic group of order $15$ is Euler's totient $\varphi(15)$. Since $15=3\cdot5$,  
  $$\varphi(15)=\varphi(3)\varphi(5)=2\cdot4=8.$$
  
- Therefore each cyclic subgroup of order $15$ contains exactly **8** elements of order $15$ (the generators of that subgroup).



### Step 2: How elements of order 15 are grouped

- Elements of order $15$ are precisely the generators of cyclic subgroups of order $15$.
  
- Two distinct cyclic subgroups of order $15$ cannot share a generator (otherwise they would be the same subgroup). They may intersect in a proper subgroup of order $1$, $3$, or $5$, but such intersections do **not** contain any element of order $15$.
  
- Hence the elements of order $15$ are partitioned into disjoint blocks of size $8$, one block for each distinct cyclic subgroup of order $15$.



### Step 3: Answering the statements

- Statement 1: If a group has an element of order $15$, then there is at least one cyclic subgroup of order $15$, so there are at least $8$ elements of order $15$.  

  Conclusion: **Statement 1 is true.**
  

- Statement 2: If a group has more than $8$ elements of order $15$, the number of such elements must be a multiple of $8$ greater than $8$, so it is at least $16$.  

  Conclusion: **Statement 2 is true.**


### Final Answer

- **Statement 1: True.**  
- **Statement 2: True.**
