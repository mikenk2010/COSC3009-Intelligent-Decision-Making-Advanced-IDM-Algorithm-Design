📝 Question

Janet's ducks lay eggs. She gets 3 times as many eggs from her ducks as she gets from her chickens. If she gets 3 eggs from her chickens, how many eggs does she get from her ducks?

Round 0
🤖 Agent A - Initial Answer
**Step 1: Identify the given information**
- Janet gets 3 eggs from her chickens.
- The number of eggs from her ducks is 3 times the number of eggs from her chickens.

**Step 2: Set up the relationship between the number of eggs from ducks and chickens**

Let's denote:
- \( E_{chickens} \) as the number of eggs from chickens.
- \( E_{ducks} \) as the number of eggs from ducks.

Given that:
\[ E_{chickens} = 3 \]

And we are told that:
\[ E_{ducks} = 3 \times E_{chickens} \]

**Step 3: Substitute and calculate**

Substitute \( E_{chickens} = 3 \) into the equation for \( E_{ducks}\):

\[ E_{ducks} = 3 \times 3 \]

\[ E_{ducks} = 9 \]

So, Janet gets **9 eggs** from her ducks.

To summarize:
- From Step 1: She gets 3 eggs from chickens.
- From Step 2 and Step 3: The relationship is \( E_{ducks} = 3 \times E_{chickens} \).
- Substitute \( E_{chickens} = 3 \) to find \( E_{ducks} = 9 \).

Therefore, the number of eggs Janet gets from her ducks is **9**.
