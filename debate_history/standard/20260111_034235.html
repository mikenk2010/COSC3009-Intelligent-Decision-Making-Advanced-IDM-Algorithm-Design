📝 Question

Janet's ducks lay eggs. She gets 3 times as many eggs from her ducks as she gets from her chickens. If she gets 3 eggs from her chickens, how many eggs does she get from her ducks?

Round 0
🤖 Agent A - Initial Answer
### Step 1: Define Variables and Given Information

Let:
- \( C \) represent the number of eggs laid by Janet's chickens.
- \( D \) represent the number of eggs laid by Janet's ducks.

According to the problem, we know:
1. Janet gets 3 times as many eggs from her ducks as she does from her chickens.
2. She gets 3 eggs from her chickens (\( C = 3 \)).

### Step 2: Express Relationship Between Eggs

Given that Janet gets 3 times as many eggs from her ducks as from her chickens, we can write:
\[ D = 3C \]

### Step 3: Substitute the Given Value for \( C \)

We know \( C = 3 \). Substituting this value into the equation gives:
\[ D = 3 \times 3 \]

\[ D = 9 \]

### Step 4: Conclusion

Janet gets 9 eggs from her ducks.

To verify, we can check that 3 times 9 equals 27, which is indeed correct based on the problem statement:
- If \( C = 3 \), then Janet would get \( 3C = 3 \times 3 = 9 \) eggs.
- Since she gets 3 times as many from her ducks as chickens, if she got 27 eggs in total, it matches the given condition. 

Thus, the final answer is:
\[ \boxed{9} \]
