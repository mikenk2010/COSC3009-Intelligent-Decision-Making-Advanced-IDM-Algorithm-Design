### Step 2: Calculate Degrees of Individual Extensions

First, let's consider each individual square root as a separate extension:
- The degree of the field extension \( \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{a}) \) over \( \mathbb{Q} \) is 2 for any rational number \( a \neq 0 \). This is because we can find an element (say, \( \sqrt{a} \)) that generates this extension.

### Step 3: Consider Nested Extensions

Next, we need to consider the nested fields:
- We know that \( \sqrt{18} = \sqrt{9 \cdot 2} = \sqrt{9} \cdot \sqrt{2} = 3\sqrt{2} \).
  
Now, let's combine all extensions:

- Since \( \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{18}) = \mathbb{Q}(3\sqrt{2}) \) and both contain \( \sqrt{2} \), the extension is even more complex. 

### Step 4: Use Tower Law

To find the degree of the composite field:
- Suppose we have a tower of fields:

  \[

  \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{18}) \subset \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2},\sqrt{3})

  \]

The tower law states that the degree of a composite extension is the product of the degrees of each individual extension:
  
- Let \( [M: N] \) denote the degree of field extension from field \( N \) to \( M \).

Thus, applying this rule:

\[ [\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{18},\sqrt{3}) : \mathbb{Q}] = [\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{18},\sqrt{2}) : \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{18})] \times [\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{18}) : \mathbb{Q}] \]

### Step 5: Calculate the Degrees

- \( [\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{18},\sqrt{3}) : \mathbb{Q}(3\sqrt{2})] = [ \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{18},\sqrt{3}) : \mathbb{Q}] - [ \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{18},\sqrt{3}) : \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{18}) ] \)

  Since \( [\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{18},\sqrt{3}) : \mathbb{Q}] = 4 \) and 

  \[ [\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{18},\sqrt{3}) : \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{18}) ] = 2 \]

\[ [\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{18},\sqrt{3}) : \mathbb{Q}] = 4 \times 2 = 8 \]
